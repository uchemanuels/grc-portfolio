# Third-Party Cybersecurity Risk Assessment — Horizon Labs

## Project Overview

This project involved conducting a third-party cybersecurity risk assessment of Horizon Labs, a critical Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) supplier to Oscorp, as part of the GRC Mastery professional training programme.

The objective was to evaluate the supplier's cybersecurity controls, identify significant security risks and provide actionable recommendations to support senior management decision-making.

| Project Information | Details |
|---|---|
| Organization | Oscorp (Simulated Enterprise) |
| Supplier | Horizon Labs |

| Recommended Supplier Risk Rating | High |
| Training Provider | GRC Mastery |

## Business Problem

Oscorp relies on Horizon Labs to provide a SaaS application used for scientific data analysis.

Given the importance of scientific research data and intellectual property, weaknesses in the supplier's cybersecurity controls could expose Oscorp to data breaches, unauthorized access, operational disruption and reputational damage.

Oscorp required a structured assessment of the supplier's security posture to identify material risks requiring senior management attention.

## Project Objectives

- Evaluate the supplier's cybersecurity controls.
- Identify significant control deficiencies.
- Assess potential security and business impacts on Oscorp.
- Recognize existing effective security controls.
- Prioritize risks requiring management escalation.
- Develop risk treatment and remediation recommendations.
- Recommend an overall supplier risk classification.

## Assessment Methodology

The assessment followed a structured questionnaire-based approach:

1. Review the completed supplier security questionnaire.
2. Evaluate the supplier's reported cybersecurity controls.
3. Identify control deficiencies and inadequately demonstrated capabilities.
4. Assess potential business impacts on Oscorp.
5. Prioritize identified risks according to their potential severity.
6. Develop remediation and risk treatment recommendations.
7. Prepare a management-level risk escalation report.

The assessment assumes that sufficient supporting evidence was provided by Horizon Labs, as stipulated in the original training scenario.

## Key Risk Findings

| Risk Area | Identified Deficiency | Recommended Priority |
|---|---|---|
| Identity and Access Management | Weak access governance and absence of regular access reviews. | Critical |
| Vulnerability Management | Absence of vulnerability scanning and penetration testing. | High |
| Incident Detection and Response | Inadequately demonstrated incident response and security monitoring capabilities. | High |
| Data Protection | Absence of data loss prevention controls. | High |
| Cybersecurity Governance | Insufficient formal security governance arrangements. | High |
| Security Awareness | Limited employee cybersecurity awareness training. | Medium |

## Potential Business Impact

The identified deficiencies could expose Oscorp to:

- Unauthorized access to scientific research data.
- Loss or disclosure of intellectual property.
- Exploitation of unaddressed security vulnerabilities.
- Delayed detection and containment of cybersecurity incidents.
- Disruption of critical scientific data analysis services.
- Regulatory, contractual and reputational consequences.

These represent potential risk exposures rather than confirmed security incidents.

## Existing Security Strengths

The assessment also identified positive security controls reported by Horizon Labs:

- Encryption controls.
- Two-factor authentication.
- Regular data backups.

These controls provide a foundation for cybersecurity improvement but do not sufficiently mitigate the significant deficiencies identified during the assessment.

## Overall Supplier Risk Classification

**Recommended Risk Rating: High**

Horizon Labs should be classified as a high-risk supplier until significant cybersecurity deficiencies have been addressed and remediation evidence has been reviewed.

The supplier's importance to Oscorp's scientific operations increases the potential business impact of inadequate security controls.

Continued engagement should be subject to appropriate risk treatment, enhanced oversight and documented management acceptance of unresolved risks.

## Recommended Remediation Roadmap

### Phase 1 — Immediate Risk Reduction (0–30 Days)

- Escalate significant supplier risks to Oscorp's senior management.
- Review privileged access and existing user permissions.
- Agree on a formal supplier remediation plan.
- Establish accountable owners and remediation deadlines.
- Confirm incident escalation and communication arrangements.

### Phase 2 — Security Control Improvement (31–90 Days)

- Establish a formal vulnerability management process.
- Introduce regular access reviews.
- Develop documented incident response procedures.
- Strengthen security monitoring capabilities.
- Improve employee cybersecurity awareness training.
- Establish formal cybersecurity governance responsibilities.

### Phase 3 — Assurance and Continuous Monitoring (91–180 Days)

- Conduct penetration testing and address identified vulnerabilities.
- Strengthen data loss prevention capabilities.
- Review evidence of remediation and control effectiveness.
- Reassess the supplier's cybersecurity risk classification.
- Establish ongoing supplier security monitoring and periodic reassessment.

The proposed remediation timelines should be agreed with the supplier and adjusted according to business criticality, risk exposure and implementation feasibility.

## Management Recommendations

Oscorp's senior management should:

- Require Horizon Labs to implement a time-bound remediation plan.
- Assign an accountable internal supplier risk owner.
- Review remediation progress regularly.
- Require supporting evidence before closing identified risk items.
- Document formal risk acceptance for unresolved material risks.
- Reassess the supplier relationship if significant risks remain unacceptable.

## Project Attribution and Limitations

This project was completed as a scenario-based practical assessment through GRC Mastery.

The original business scenario and supplier security questionnaire were supplied by the training provider. The portfolio report expands the original submitted assessment using AI-assisted analysis and documentation.

The assessment assumes that sufficient supporting evidence was provided, in accordance with the training scenario. No independent technical testing, penetration testing or verification of supplier controls was performed.

Risk classifications, remediation timelines and management recommendations represent proposed professional judgments rather than confirmed supplier commitments or implemented security improvements.

This project does not represent an actual supplier engagement or independent security audit.

The original training materials and supplier questionnaire are excluded from this repository.
