# IT Asset Management and CMDB Process Design

## Project Overview

This project involved designing an IT Asset Management and Configuration Management Database (CMDB) process for Oscorp, a simulated enterprise, as part of the GRC Mastery professional training programme.

The objective was to address internal audit findings relating to inadequate asset management practices and the absence of an accurate, centralized asset management database.

## Business Problem

Following a cybersecurity internal audit, Oscorp identified significant deficiencies in its asset management practices.

The organization relied on a single spreadsheet containing ad hoc IT system information and lacked an accurate, centralized asset management database.

This limited its ability to maintain asset visibility, establish ownership, identify critical assets, manage configuration dependencies and assess cybersecurity risks effectively.

## Project Objectives

- Establish a centralized and accurate asset inventory.
- Design a structured Configuration Management Database.
- Identify and classify organizational assets.
- Identify and prioritize crown jewel assets.
- Establish asset ownership and accountability.
- Automate asset discovery and reconciliation.
- Manage assets throughout their lifecycle.
- Establish measurable controls and audit evidence requirements.

## Proposed Solution

The proposed asset management process consists of eight complementary components.

| Component | Proposed Solution |
|---|---|
| Asset Discovery | Identify hardware, software, cloud resources, data assets and business services. |
| Asset Classification | Categorize assets according to sensitivity, criticality and business impact. |
| Crown Jewel Identification | Identify critical information assets and map their supporting dependencies. |
| Centralized CMDB | Migrate existing spreadsheet records into a governed configuration management database. |
| Asset Ownership | Assign accountable business owners and technical custodians. |
| Automation | Integrate approved discovery tools and authoritative data sources. |
| Control Monitoring | Conduct periodic reconciliation, owner attestations and exception reviews. |
| Lifecycle Management | Track assets from acquisition through retirement and secure disposal. |

## Asset Management Lifecycle

The proposed process establishes six lifecycle stages:

1. **Request and Acquisition:** Register approved asset acquisitions and assign provisional ownership.
2. **Discovery and Import:** Identify assets through approved discovery sources and existing records.
3. **Validation and Classification:** Verify asset information, ownership, sensitivity and criticality.
4. **Registration and Relationship Mapping:** Create CMDB records and document configuration dependencies.
5. **Operation and Change:** Maintain accurate records through approved changes and automated reconciliation.
6. **Retirement and Disposal:** Securely retire assets, revoke access and retain disposal evidence.

## CMDB Record Design

The proposed CMDB captures the following information:

- Unique asset and configuration item identifiers.
- Asset name, category and configuration details.
- Business owner and technical custodian.
- Physical or cloud location.
- Business criticality and information classification.
- Lifecycle and operational status.
- Associated business services and dependencies.
- Discovery and verification timestamps.
- Change records and exception status.
- Retirement and disposal evidence.

The CMDB also supports configuration relationship mapping to improve incident impact analysis, change management and business continuity planning.

## Governance and Accountability

The proposed governance model establishes responsibilities for:

- IT Asset and CMDB Manager.
- Business and asset owners.
- IT Operations and Procurement.
- Governance, Risk and Compliance.
- Cybersecurity teams.
- Internal Audit.

This separates operational ownership, risk oversight and independent assurance responsibilities.

| Phase | Timeline | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 | Days 0–30 | Governance, asset discovery planning, data standards and crown jewel identification. |
| Phase 2 | Days 31–60 | CMDB configuration, data cleansing, migration and discovery integration. |
| Phase 3 | Days 61–90 | Expanded coverage, owner training, reconciliation and management acceptance. |
| Continuous Improvement | Ongoing | Monitoring, periodic reviews, exception management and independent assurance. |

## Proposed Performance Indicators

| Indicator | Proposed Target |
|---|---|
| Asset inventory coverage | At least 95% of independently discovered in-scope assets |
| Crown jewel coverage | 100% of identified crown jewels |
| Critical asset ownership | 100% |
| Overall asset ownership | At least 95% |
| Asset record reconciliation | Monthly |
| Configuration change accuracy | At least 95% within two business days |
| Retirement and disposal evidence | 100% of sampled retired assets |

These targets are proposed initial performance measures rather than achieved results.

## Project Attribution and Limitations

This project was completed as a scenario-based practical assessment through GRC Mastery.

The portfolio report expands the original submitted assessment using AI-assisted analysis and documentation.

Oscorp is a simulated organization. The proposed processes, configuration records, implementation timelines and performance targets represent design recommendations rather than existing or implemented organizational capabilities.

No actual CMDB deployment, asset discovery exercise or independent audit testing was performed.

The original training materials and official assessment solutions are excluded from this repository.
